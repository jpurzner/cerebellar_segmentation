Synthetic IGL Study

Which equations and which parameter values produce an IGL that looks like the real one? Real IGLs (your controls and the Allen P7 Nissl series) were measured with one set of 20 statistics; 131,000 synthetic sections were measured the same way: 59,000 drawn at random from seven generative models, then 72,000 from a local search around the closest ones. A synthetic section counts as sensible only when it is as typical as the real sections are of each other. Your EZH2 cKO sections were used as a third target, to see which parameters move an IGL from the control look to the cKO look.

    Smoothed approximations of the real IGL

    Each real IGL rebuilt from its own EGL and white matter with as few numbers as possible. One number: the IGL is all tissue that is not white matter and lies deeper than an ML thickness m below the EGL. Two numbers: the IGL is the band a ≤ depth ≤ b below the EGL, no white matter used. The smooth versions let those depths drift with position, averaged over 50–800 µm. Scores are Dice with the real IGL.

    How the layers scale with folium size

    Along the centre line of every folium (the medial axis of the tissue inside the EGL), the half-width h, from centre to EGL, splits into ML, IGL and white matter. Median of all centre-line points in 25 µm bins of h; shaded band = interquartile range. This is the rule a generator has to reproduce.

    Seven ways to generate an IGL

    Folded surface (fold). A dome-shaped outline is cut by fissures running from the surface toward a core near the base: a few deep main fissures, more shallow minor ones, each bent and open as a wedge at the surface. Every layer is a depth below the pial surface: EGL from 0 to e, ML from e to e + m, IGL from e + m to e + m + g, white matter deeper. Thicknesses vary smoothly (CV, correlation length), with the folding (k), and at cell scale (roughness).

    Folded surface with core options (fold2). The same, with two structural options the sampling can switch on or off: fissures aim at an inner core ellipse of relative size core_r instead of one point (with 0, deep fissures converge and pinch the tissue between them), and in narrow folia the EGL and ML may take at most a share 1 − q of the folium's half-width, so every folium keeps an IGL core.

    Core options, corrected (fold3). fold2 turned out to strip the EGL off the surface in two ways: the folium half-width was read from the nearest point of the medial axis, which has short spurs toward every bump of the outline, so near the surface the half-width was far too small and the EGL and ML were squeezed away; and the depth jitter moved the EGL boundary along with the deeper ones. fold3 uses the local thickness (the largest disk inside the tissue that contains the pixel) and jitters only the ML, IGL and white-matter boundaries. Added during the night.

    Folded surface with medial white matter (foldma). The folded surface again, but the white matter is a tree along the folia's medial axis: it exists only where a folium's half-width h exceeds h_min, and its radius is a share ρ of the room left under the EGL and ML (h − e − m). The IGL fills everything between the ML and that tree, so its thickness follows the room in each folium instead of being a parameter. This model was added during the night after the first two failed in the same way (see below).

    Fingers (finger). Lobules as parallel-sided fingers on a central core instead of wedges cut into a dome: each lobule is a capsule of half-width W and length L, and some split at the tip into two or three narrower sub-folia. Fissures are the narrow gaps between neighbouring fingers. Layers by depth as in fold. Added during the night after the measured layer rule (above) showed that wedge-shaped lobules, which narrow toward the core, are what breaks the synthetic IGL apart.

    Edge roughness. Real IGL edges wiggle at the scale of a few cells, which gives the ribbon its curvature. The first families roughen each layer's thickness separately, which can pinch the IGL into pieces; fold2, fold3 and finger instead shift the layer boundaries together by a smooth random depth offset (jit, 24 µm correlation), which wiggles the IGL without pinching it.

    White-matter tree (arbor). The arbor vitae is drawn first, as tapering branches from a core, some splitting into folia; tissue covers the tree and fissures run between neighbouring branches. EGL and ML are depths below the surface, and the IGL fills everything between the ML and the tree. The IGL's thickness is not a parameter: it is whatever is left.

    Smooth surface (fourier). A circle deformed by random Fourier modes, no fissures, layers by depth. A control: can folding without fissures make a real IGL?

    What separates the targets in parameter space

    For a model that reached sections like two targets, the parameters of the passing sections compared: median and interquartile range for each, and the shift between the medians in units of the average IQR. Large shifts are the parameters that move an IGL from one group's look to the other's; small shifts are shared.
