IGL Ribbon Model

The internal granular layer is drawn as a ribbon: a smooth centre line that carries a width and sits at its own depth below the EGL. A missing stretch is predicted as the curve that bends least while staying at that depth, joined to both sides with matching directions. Everything below is drawn from your sections.

How a ribbon is described

The IGL label is cleaned and skeletonised on a 4 µm grid. Each skeleton branch becomes a centre line γ(s), where s is distance along the ribbon in µm. Three quantities are read along it: the curvature κ(s), how fast the direction turns; the width w(s), twice the distance from the centre line to the IGL edge; and the depth d(s), the distance from the centre line to the nearest EGL. Correlation length is how far along the ribbon a value stays predictable: the distance at which the correlation of a quantity with itself falls to 1/e. Width and depth change over about 50 µm. Curvature decorrelates in 12 µm, which is three grid pixels, so part of that is pixel noise in the skeleton; the large-scale bends of the folia are carried by the depth term below.

The equations

A gap has two flank ends, a and b. From the last 200 µm of IGL on each side the model takes the end position, the direction the ribbon is heading over its last 60 µm (ta, tb), and the median width and depth 40–200 µm behind the end (wa, wb, da, db). The predicted centre line is the curve that minimises an energy:

BendingPenalises turning. A straight bridge costs nothing; a sharp kink costs a lot. Divided by the curvature intensity of real ribbons, so turning as much as an intact ribbon does is cheap.
DepthKeeps the curve at the ribbon's depth below the EGL, blended linearly from one end's depth to the other's. This is what makes a bridge follow the folium surface around a tip instead of cutting across it.
BarrierPushes the curve out of EGL, white matter and background, keeping at least m = 8 µm away. Inside regions you confirmed as bleached, white matter is not a barrier, because bleach is often labelled white matter.
Even spacingKeeps the points along the curve evenly spaced so that the curvature estimate stays honest. It does not change the shape.

Why these terms

Read as a probability, p(γ | flanks) ∝ e−E[γ]. The bending term is what you get if the curvature were random noise of intensity q, which makes the heading drift like a random walk; the depth term says the true depth wanders around the blended value with a spread σd and a correlation length ℓd. Solving it

The curve is a chain of points γ0 … γn about h = 8 µm apart, with γ0 = a and γn = b fixed. Two ghost points outside the ends hold the end directions. Curvature becomes a second difference, and the energy is minimised over the inner points with L-BFGS-B, using the exact gradient.

The minimisation starts from two different curves and keeps whichever ends with the lower energy. The first is the smooth cubic that leaves a along ta and arrives at b along tb. The second is the cheapest route at the ribbon's depth: a shortest path on the grid where each step costs more the further it strays from the mean end depth, and EGL, white matter and background cannot be crossed. The second start is what finds the way around a folium tip when the two ends of a gap face each other across a thin core of white matter.

From centre line to IGL

The width is blended smoothly from one end to the other, and every pixel within half that width of the centre line becomes IGL, as long as it is ML, PCL, unassigned, a non-tear anomaly, or white matter inside a confirmed region. EGL is never painted.

Turning the weights

The folium-tip gap again, solved with other weights. A small depth tolerance σd holds the curve tightly to the blended depth; with the depth term off, only bending is left and the curve cuts the corner. The bending scale β sets how stiff the ribbon is: smaller β makes turning more expensive. The model uses σd = 15 µm and β = 0.3, the setting with the lowest median error on the tuning gaps; on this particular gap a stiffer setting does even better.

How well it predicts

180 stretches of 50–500 µm were cut from the five controls, one at a time. Half were used to choose the weights; the 90 held out are scored here. A prediction counts as right when its centre line lies on average within 20 µm of the true one, about a third of the ribbon's width. When the old repair produces no bridge at all, it counts as wrong.

Width is the weak part. The width blended between the two ends misses the true width by 19 µm on average, no better than a constant: width changes over about 50 µm of ribbon, so the ends say little about the middle of a long gap. The centre line, which is what decides whether the ribbon is continuous, is predicted well up to about 350 µm and usually up to 500 µm.
